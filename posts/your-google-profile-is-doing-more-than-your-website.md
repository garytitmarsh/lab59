# Your Google Business Profile Is Doing More Than Your Website

*By Gary Titmarsh*

Ask most small business owners what gets them found online and they'll say "my website." Fair guess, but for local businesses it's usually wrong. The thing doing most of the work is their Google Business Profile, the listing with your name, hours, reviews, and map pin that shows up when someone searches for what you do nearby.

Someone looking for a plumber, a hairdresser, or a local cafe isn't typing your business name into Google. They're searching "plumber near me" or "cafe open now," and what comes back is a map with a handful of listings. That's your Business Profile doing the work of getting you seen, before your website even gets a look in.

## Why this matters more than most people realise

A good website with no active Business Profile is like a shopfront on a street nobody walks down. It might look great once someone finds it, but it's not doing much to get found in the first place.

A Business Profile that's active, accurate, and getting reviews will often bring in more genuine local enquiries than a website ever will on its own. It's free, it's specifically built for local search, and Google actively rewards profiles that are kept up to date.

## What "active" actually means

It's not complicated, but it does need attention:

- Correct hours, especially around public holidays
- Photos that are current and actually yours
- Replying to reviews, good and bad
- Posting the occasional update when something changes

None of this needs a marketing budget. It needs about twenty minutes a month and the discipline to keep it accurate.

## Where the website fits in

This is why we don't sell the website as the centrepiece. It's the credibility check that happens after someone finds you, not the thing that gets you found. The Business Profile does the heavy lifting of visibility. The website closes the loop by confirming you're legitimate once they click through.

Get the profile right first. The website matters, but it's playing a supporting role, not the lead.
