# The Website You Won't Have to Think About Again

*By Gary Titmarsh*

I've lost count of the calls I used to field from small business owners whose site had just broken. A plugin update wiped their layout. Their hosting renewal lapsed and nobody noticed for three weeks. Their old web guy stopped answering emails and now nobody has the login.

None of that is really about websites. It's about the kind of website they were sold.

## Why most small business sites need constant attention

Platforms like WordPress run on a database, a stack of plugins, and a server that has to process every single page load. That's genuinely useful if you're running a site with logins, e-commerce, or content that changes constantly. It's a lot of moving parts for a business that just needs to show people who they are and how to get in touch.

Every one of those moving parts is something that can go wrong. Plugins need updating, and updating them can break the layout. Themes go unsupported. Hosting needs renewing. None of it is difficult exactly, it's just ongoing, and it's easy to lose track of until something stops working.

## What a static site does differently

A static site is just HTML, CSS, and images, no database, no plugins, nothing running behind the scenes. It's hosted directly on infrastructure like AWS or Cloudflare, which means it loads fast and stays up without anyone tending to it.

There's no login to lose, no update to miss, no plugin conflict to untangle. It sits there and works, the same way today as it did the day it was built.

That's not a sales pitch, it's just how the technology works. Static files don't decay the way dynamic systems do.

## Who this is right for

This isn't the right approach for every business. If you're selling products online, taking bookings through your site, or publishing new content every week, you need something with more moving parts, and that's a fair trade for what it gives you.

But if your website's job is to be found, look legitimate, and tell people how to reach you, a static build gives you that without asking anything of you afterward. You won't need to log in to update a plugin. You won't get an email saying your site's been flagged for a security patch. It just works, in the background, while you get on with running your business.
